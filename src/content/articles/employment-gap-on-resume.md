Gaps happen: layoffs, health, family, study, travel, a business that didn't work out. What worries recruiters isn't the gap; it's not knowing what happened.

## When to mention it

- **Under 3–6 months:** usually no explanation needed.
- **Over 6 months:** add a line in your timeline.
- **Over 2 years:** add the line, and show what keeps your skills current.

## How to show it

Put it in your experience section, in date order, like a short entry:

```
Career break | Mar 2023 – Jun 2024
Full-time caregiving for a family member. Completed Google Data Analytics certificate.
```

## One-line explanations

| Reason | Line |
| --- | --- |
| Layoff | "Role made redundant in company restructuring; job search and upskilling in cloud fundamentals." |
| Caregiving | "Career break: full-time caregiving for a family member." |
| Parental leave | "Career break: parental leave." |
| Health | "Career break: health reasons, now fully resolved." (No details needed.) |
| Study | "Full-time study: MBA, XYZ University." |
| Travel | "Career break: travelled across Southeast Asia; freelance writing for two travel sites." |
| Startup | "Founder, [Name]: built and ran a D2C snacks brand; closed after 18 months." |
| Exam preparation | "Full-time preparation for civil services examinations." |

## Fill it if you can

Courses, certifications, freelance work, volunteering, family business work or a personal project all show momentum. List them under the gap entry or in their own sections.

## In the interview

Say it in two sentences, then move to why you're ready now: "I took eighteen months off to care for my father. He's stable now, and I spent the last six months refreshing my SQL and Power BI skills, so I'm ready to get back to analytics."
