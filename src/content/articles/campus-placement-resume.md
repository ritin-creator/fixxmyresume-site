## How shortlisting usually works

1. **Eligibility filter:** CGPA or percentage cut-offs, active backlogs, branch.
2. **Resume screen:** projects, internships, skills, achievements.
3. **Tests and interviews:** aptitude, coding or group discussion, then interviews based on your resume.

You can't change step 1 at this point, but steps 2 and 3 depend heavily on your resume.

## Follow the college format

Many placement cells use a fixed template so recruiters can compare candidates. Use it. Within it, everything below still applies.

## What to include

**Education:** degree, branch, college, year, CGPA; class XII and X with board and percentage. Mention backlogs honestly if the form asks.

**Projects (most important for tech roles):** two or three, each with tools and a result. See [project section guide](/articles/project-section-in-fresher-resume/).

**Internships:** company, role, dates, two bullets with outcomes.

**Positions of responsibility:** club head, fest coordinator, class representative, placement coordinator, with scale.

> Sponsorship Head, TechFest 2025 | raised ₹6 lakh from 14 sponsors, up from ₹3.5 lakh the year before

**Achievements:** hackathon results, competitive programming ratings, scholarships, olympiad ranks, NPTEL toppers.

**Skills:** tools you can be tested on.

## Prepare for every line

Interviewers pick lines from your resume and ask you to explain them. If you can't talk about a project or tool for five minutes, take it off.

## Mistakes in placement resumes

- Every project described as "worked on"
- Listing 15 programming languages
- Generic objective
- Two pages
- Fake internships or certificates; background checks find them
