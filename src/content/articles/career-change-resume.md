A career changer's problem isn't a lack of experience; it's experience written for the wrong reader. A recruiter in your new field has to see the connection in a few seconds.

## 1. Lead with where you're going

> Former sales manager moving into product management. 6 years turning customer feedback into feature requests, 3 of which became top-requested features. Completed a product management certificate and shipped a side project used by 400 people.

## 2. Use a hybrid format

After the summary, add a "Key skills" block that combines transferable skills and new ones, then your full work history with dates. See [format comparison](/articles/resume-formats-chronological-functional-hybrid/).

## 3. Find your transferable skills

| From | To | Transferable |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Teaching | Instructional design, training | Curriculum design, assessments, presenting, managing groups |
| Sales | Product, customer success | Customer research, negotiation, pipeline data, stakeholder management |
| Journalism | Communications, content | Writing on deadline, interviewing, editing, research |
| Engineering | Data analytics | Problem-solving, statistics, process data, tools like Python or MATLAB |
| Hospitality | Customer support, operations | Service recovery, scheduling, handling volume under pressure |
| Military | Operations, logistics | Leading teams, planning, equipment and supply management |

## 4. Rewrite old bullets for the new reader

Translate jargon and pick the bullets that matter in the new field.

**Old (for teaching):** "Delivered CBSE class 9 science syllabus to three sections."

**New (for instructional design):** "Designed 40+ lesson plans and assessments a year for 120 students; redesigned a unit that lifted average test scores by 11%."

## 5. Add proof from the new field

- A recognised course or certificate
- One to three projects, freelance gigs or volunteer work in the new area
- Portfolio or GitHub links

## 6. Keep older, unrelated detail short

Roles that don't support the switch can shrink to one line.

## What to avoid

- A functional resume that hides dates
- Apologising for the change
- Listing every course you've started

Write your cover letter around the "why": see [how to write a cover letter](/articles/how-to-write-a-cover-letter/).
