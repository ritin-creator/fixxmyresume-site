## The difference

| | Objective | Summary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Focus | What you want | What you offer |
| Typical user | Freshers, career changers | Everyone with some experience |
| Length | 1–2 lines | 2–3 lines |
| Example | "Seeking an entry-level analyst role where I can apply SQL and statistics." | "Data analyst with 3 years in retail, skilled in SQL and Power BI; built dashboards used by 40 managers." |

## Why summaries won

Recruiters read the top of a resume to decide if they should keep going. A summary answers that question directly; an objective makes them work it out. Objectives also tend to be generic, so they waste the most valuable space on the page.

## When an objective still works

- You're a fresher with little to summarise.
- You're changing field and need to say so up front.
- You're applying to a specific programme (a trainee scheme, a campus role).

Make it specific: name the role, the field and one thing you bring.

**Weak:** "To obtain a challenging position in a reputed organisation that offers growth."

**Better:** "Commerce graduate seeking an accounts executive role in manufacturing, bringing a 6-month internship in accounts payable and working knowledge of Tally and GST."

## A hybrid that works

Write a summary, and let the last clause point to the target: "…now looking to move into product management." That gives you both.
