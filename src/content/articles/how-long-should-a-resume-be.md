Length isn't a rule, it's a budget. Recruiters skim, so every line should earn its place.

## Rule of thumb by experience

| Experience | Length | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Student, fresher, 0–2 years | 1 page | Not enough relevant material to fill two well |
| 2–8 years | 1 page, 2 if dense with relevant work | Most mid-level resumes fit on one page with tight bullets |
| 8+ years, senior or managerial | 2 pages | Room for scope, leadership and results across roles |
| Academic, research, medical | As long as needed | Publications, grants and clinical work are expected in full |

## Country norms

- **US:** one page is the strong default for early career; two for senior.
- **UK, Ireland, Australia, New Zealand:** a "CV" of two pages is normal.
- **India:** one page for freshers; one to two for experienced professionals. Long biodata-style CVs are fading in corporate hiring. See [biodata vs resume vs CV](/articles/biodata-vs-resume-vs-cv/).
- **Europe:** often two pages; some countries have their own conventions (see [CV vs resume](/articles/resume-vs-cv-difference/)).

## How to cut a long resume

1. Keep detailed bullets only for the last 10 to 15 years.
2. Collapse older jobs into one line: title, company, years.
3. Remove duties anyone in the role would do; keep achievements.
4. Merge similar bullets.
5. Drop the objective statement if you have a summary.
6. Remove hobbies unless they're relevant to the job.

## How to fill a thin one-pager

Add projects, internships, volunteering, certifications or relevant coursework. A fresher with strong projects beats one with padding. See [resume with no experience](/articles/resume-with-no-experience/).
