> In India, see [Biodata vs Resume vs CV](/articles/biodata-vs-resume-vs-cv/) for how the terms are used there.


"CV" is short for *curriculum vitae*, Latin for "course of life". Whether it means something different from a resume depends on where you are.

## The two meanings

| | Resume (US sense) | Academic CV |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Length | 1–2 pages | As long as needed |
| Purpose | Get a job interview | Full record for academic, research or medical roles |
| Content | Selected, tailored achievements | Everything: publications, grants, teaching, conferences |
| Tailored per job | Yes | Mostly the same each time |

## By country

| Country or region | Word used | What it means |
| --- | --- | --- |
| US, Canada | Resume (CV for academia) | 1–2 page tailored document |
| UK, Ireland | CV | 2-page standard document, like a US resume |
| Europe | CV | 1–2 pages; the Europass format exists but many employers prefer a simple CV |
| India | Resume or CV, used interchangeably | 1–2 pages; "biodata" is an older, personal-details-heavy format ([comparison](/articles/biodata-vs-resume-vs-cv/)) |
| UAE, Saudi Arabia, Gulf | CV | Often 2 pages; personal details like nationality and visa status are common ([Gulf guide](/articles/gulf-job-resume-uae-saudi/)) |
| Australia, New Zealand | CV or resume | 2–3 pages is normal |

## Which should you send?

Use the word the job advert uses, and match the local length norm. If you're applying in the US with a UK-style CV, trim it to a tailored resume. If you're applying for a professorship anywhere, send an academic CV.

Content-wise, the rules for a good document are the same everywhere: clear layout, achievements with numbers, and keywords that match the job. See [how to write a resume](/articles/how-to-write-a-resume/).
