For freshers, projects are the main evidence of skill. Interviewers often spend most of the technical round on them.

## Choose the right projects

- Closest to the job you're applying for
- Ones you did a real part of, not just attended
- Ones with a result you can describe
- Two or three is enough

## Four-part format

**Title | Tools | (Link)**
• What you built or analysed, and for whom
• The result, with a number

## Examples

**IT**
> **Placement Portal API** | Java, Spring Boot, MySQL | github.com/user/portal
> • Built REST APIs for 900 students and 40 recruiters to post and apply to drives
> • Added email alerts and role-based login, saving the placement cell about 10 hours a week

**Data**
> **Bike-share Demand Analysis** | SQL, Tableau
> • Analysed 120,000 trips to find stations that run out of bikes at peak hours
> • Recommended moving 15 bikes between 6 stations, which would cover most shortages

**Commerce**
> **Financial Analysis of Two FMCG Companies** | Excel
> • Compared 5 years of ratios (liquidity, profitability, leverage) from annual reports
> • Presented a hold/buy view to the class; scored top marks in the assignment

**Marketing**
> **College Fest Social Media** | Instagram, Canva, Meta Ads
> • Planned 40 posts and reels over 4 weeks; reached 25,000 accounts
> • Ran a ₹3,000 ad test that brought 400 ticket sign-ups

**Mechanical**
> **Fixture Redesign (internship)** | SolidWorks
> • Redesigned a drilling fixture for a gearbox housing
> • Cut setup time by 15% on one machine

## Team projects

Say what *you* did: "Built the backend in a 4-person team" is better than taking credit for the whole thing.

## No numbers?

Use scale (records, users, pages), accuracy, time, marks, or a before-and-after. See [quantifying achievements](/articles/how-to-quantify-achievements-on-resume/).
