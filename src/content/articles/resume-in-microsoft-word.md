Word is still what many recruiters and agencies prefer to receive. The trick is to keep the document structurally simple.

## Step by step

1. **Open a blank document** (or a template you've confirmed is single-column with no tables).
2. **Margins:** Layout > Margins > Custom, 0.5–0.75 inch.
3. **Font:** Home tab, Calibri or Arial, 10.5–11 pt; line spacing 1.0–1.15.
4. **Name and contacts:** type them at the top of the body. Don't use Insert > Header.
5. **Headings:** type each section name and apply Heading 1 or Heading 2 from the Styles gallery. Modify the style once to change all of them.
6. **Job lines:** title and company in bold, then city and dates. Use a right tab stop for dates instead of spaces or tables.
7. **Bullets:** Home > Bullets. One to two lines each.
8. **Save:** File > Save As > Word Document (.docx) and also PDF.

## Why heading styles help

Real heading styles give the document structure that parsers, screen readers and Word's navigation pane can use. They also keep your formatting consistent.

## Common Word traps

- Templates built on invisible tables (click inside and look for "Table Design" in the ribbon)
- Text boxes and shapes for sidebars
- Columns set under Layout > Columns
- Symbols inserted as icons for phone and email

## Check it

Save as PDF, select all the text, paste it into Notepad. If the order is right and nothing is missing, you're fine. See also [PDF or Word](/articles/pdf-or-word-resume/).
