## How these drives usually work

1. **Registration** on the company's careers portal or a national hiring platform, with eligibility checks
2. **Online test:** aptitude, reasoning, verbal, coding
3. **Technical interview:** your projects, a language, DBMS, OOP, sometimes coding
4. **HR interview:** communication, relocation, shifts, joining date

The resume matters most in round 3: interviewers read it and pick questions from it.

## What to include

- **Eligibility details:** degree, branch, year of passing, CGPA, class XII and X percentages
- **Core skills:** one language you know well (Java, Python, C++), SQL, data structures, OOP, operating systems basics
- **Projects:** two or three, with your role, tools, and a result
- **Internship** (if any)
- **Certifications:** cloud fundamentals, NPTEL courses, a language certificate
- **Achievements:** coding contest ranks, hackathons

## Be ready to explain every line

Expect: "Explain your project architecture", "Why did you use MySQL instead of MongoDB?", "Write a query for the second-highest salary", "What is polymorphism?" If a skill on your resume would make you nervous to be asked about, remove it.

## Experienced professionals moving between IT services firms

Show client domain (banking, retail, telecom), technologies, team size, and outcomes: tickets resolved, SLAs met, automation hours saved, migrations delivered. Include certifications from your current employer's programmes.
