## Resume and documents

**Resume.** A short (1–2 page) document summarising your experience, skills and education for a job application.

**CV (curriculum vitae).** In the UK, Europe, India and the Gulf, the same thing as a resume; in the US, a long academic record. See [resume vs CV](/articles/resume-vs-cv-difference/).

**Biodata.** An older Indian format heavy on personal details; now mostly used for government jobs and matrimonial purposes. See [biodata vs resume](/articles/biodata-vs-resume-vs-cv/).

**Cover letter.** A short letter sent with a resume explaining why you fit the role.

**Reverse-chronological.** A format listing your most recent job first.

**Functional resume.** A format grouping skills by theme and downplaying dates; risky with ATS.

**Hybrid (combination) resume.** A skills summary followed by a full job history.

**Summary.** Two or three lines at the top of a resume stating your role, experience and strengths.

**Objective.** A line stating the job you want; mostly used by freshers.

**Headline.** A one-line title under your name or on a job portal profile.

**Portfolio.** A collection of work samples, usually online.

## Screening and software

**ATS (applicant tracking system).** Software employers use to collect, store, search and track applications. See [what is an ATS resume](/articles/what-is-an-ats-resume/).

**Parsing.** The ATS extracting your name, contacts, jobs, dates and skills from your file.

**Keyword.** A term (job title, tool, skill) recruiters search for.

**Keyword matching.** Comparing words in your resume with those in the job description.

**Knockout question.** A yes/no screening question (location, licence, notice period) that can filter you out.

**AI screening.** Software that reads and ranks resumes using AI models. See [how AI screens resumes](/articles/how-ai-screens-resumes/).

**ATS score.** A score some tools give to show how well a resume reads and matches; each tool calculates it differently.

**Job board / job portal.** A site listing jobs (Naukri, LinkedIn, Indeed, Foundit).

**Resume database.** A pool of profiles recruiters search, such as Naukri's.

**Boolean search.** Searching with AND, OR, NOT, for example `"data analyst" AND SQL NOT intern`.

## Hiring process

**JD (job description).** The advert listing duties and requirements.

**Recruiter.** The person who sources and screens candidates.

**Hiring manager.** The person you'd work for, who makes the final call.

**HR round.** An interview covering fit, salary and logistics.

**Technical round.** An interview testing job skills.

**Case study / assignment.** A take-home or live task.

**Shortlist.** Candidates moved forward after screening.

**Referral.** A current employee recommending you.

**Campus placement.** Hiring that happens through your college. See [campus placement resume](/articles/campus-placement-resume/).

**Off-campus hiring.** Fresher hiring outside college placement drives.

## Offer and joining

**CTC (cost to company).** In India, the total yearly cost of employing you, including salary and employer contributions. See [CTC on resume](/articles/ctc-and-expected-ctc-on-resume/).

**In-hand / take-home salary.** What reaches your bank account after deductions.

**Fixed and variable pay.** Guaranteed salary and performance-linked pay.

**Expected CTC.** The package you're asking for.

**Notice period.** How long you must stay at your current job after resigning. See [notice period](/articles/notice-period-on-resume/).

**Buyout.** A new employer paying to shorten your notice period.

**Offer letter.** The written job offer.

**Background verification (BGV).** Checks on your education, employment and identity.

**Relieving letter.** A letter from your old employer confirming you've left.

**Experience letter.** A letter confirming your role and dates.

**Probation.** An initial period, often 3–6 months, before confirmation.

**Appraisal.** A periodic performance review.

## Work types

**Full-time, part-time, contract, freelance.** Employment types by hours and contract.

**Remote, hybrid, on-site.** Where you work.

**Internship.** Short-term training work, paid or unpaid.

**Fresher.** In India, a candidate with no full-time experience.

**Lateral hire.** An experienced hire joining at a similar level from another company.
