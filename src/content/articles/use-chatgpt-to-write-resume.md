AI writing tools are good at structure, grammar and phrasing. They're bad at knowing what you actually did. Most weak AI resumes come from asking the tool to fill gaps it can't know.

## The rule

**You supply the facts. The AI supplies the wording.**

## Prompts that work

**Rewrite bullets:**

> Rewrite these resume bullets for a [target job title] role. Rules: use only the facts I give, don't invent numbers or tools, start each bullet with a past-tense verb, one line each, plain English, no buzzwords. Bullets: [paste your rough bullets with real numbers]

**Write a summary:**

> Write a 2-line resume summary for a [target title] role using only these facts: [years, specialities, best result]. No adjectives like "passionate" or "results-driven".

**Find missing keywords:**

> Here is a job description and my resume. List the skills and tools in the job description that are missing from my resume. Don't add them; just list them. [paste both]

**Find gaps in impact:**

> Which of these bullets lack a number or a result? For each, ask me one question that would help me add one.

## Check every line

- Is this true? Could I explain it in an interview?
- Did it add a number, tool or title I didn't give?
- Does it sound like me, or like every other resume?

Watch for phrases like "proven track record", "dynamic", "spearheaded" in every bullet, and "leveraged synergies". Replace them with what happened.

## What not to do

- Ask AI to "make up achievements" or "fill in numbers"
- Paste confidential client names, salaries or internal data into a public AI tool
- Copy a full AI resume without editing
