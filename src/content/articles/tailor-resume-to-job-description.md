Tailoring isn't rewriting. It's choosing which true things to show first, in the words this employer uses.

## Before you start: a master resume

Keep one long document with every job, project, bullet and skill you could use. Each tailored resume is a one-page selection from it.

## The 15-minute routine

**Minutes 1–3: read the advert.** Highlight the job title, the top five skills or tools, and anything repeated. Note the seniority and the problem they're hiring to solve.

**Minutes 4–5: summary.** Put their job title in your first line and name two of their top skills that you have.

**Minutes 6–8: skills.** Move their named skills to the front. Use their spelling ("Microsoft Excel" vs "Excel").

**Minutes 9–12: bullets.** For your last one or two roles, pick the bullets that prove what they asked for. Swap less relevant ones for stronger matches from your master resume.

**Minutes 13–14: cut.** Remove anything that doesn't support this application.

**Minute 15: save and name it.** `Firstname-Lastname-Company-Role.pdf`.

## Worked example

**Advert:** "Customer Success Manager, B2B SaaS. Own a book of mid-market accounts, drive renewals and expansion, run QBRs, work with product on feedback. HubSpot experience a plus."

**Untailored summary:** "Account manager with 4 years of experience in client relationships."

**Tailored:** "Customer success manager with 4 years in B2B SaaS, owning a $2.4M book of mid-market accounts with 96% renewal. Runs quarterly business reviews and feeds customer insights to product. HubSpot daily user."

**Bullets moved up:** renewals, expansion revenue, QBRs, product feedback. **Moved down or cut:** event organising, internal reporting.

## What not to change

Job titles you actually held, dates, employers and degrees. Tailor emphasis and wording, never facts. See [keywords from a job description](/articles/resume-keywords-from-job-description/).
