## Myth 1: "75% of resumes are rejected by ATS robots"

**Reality:** this figure is widely repeated without a solid source. Most ATS don't silently auto-reject; they store, filter and rank, and a person searches. Your resume can still be invisible if it parses badly or lacks the terms a recruiter searches for, which is why format and keywords matter.

## Myth 2: "A resume must be one page"

**Reality:** one page is right for most people with under about 8 years of experience. Senior professionals can use two. See [resume length](/articles/how-long-should-a-resume-be/).

## Myth 3: "Put every keyword from the job description in white text"

**Reality:** hidden text shows up when the resume is pasted or parsed, and recruiters treat it as a red flag.

## Myth 4: "A creative design helps you stand out"

**Reality:** two columns, icons and graphics often scramble in ATS. Stand out with results, not decoration. Designers can link a portfolio.

## Myth 5: "Always include an objective"

**Reality:** a specific summary works better for almost everyone. See [objective vs summary](/articles/resume-objective-vs-summary/).

## Myth 6: "List every job you've ever had"

**Reality:** 10–15 years of detail is plenty. Older roles can be a single line.

## Myth 7: "Gaps will get you rejected"

**Reality:** gaps are common. A short, honest line explaining one works better than hiding it. See [employment gaps](/articles/employment-gap-on-resume/).

## Myth 8: "PDFs can't be read by ATS"

**Reality:** text-based PDFs are read well by most modern systems. Scanned PDFs are the problem. See [PDF or Word](/articles/pdf-or-word-resume/).

## Myth 9: "Recruiters don't care about numbers for non-sales jobs"

**Reality:** numbers make any job concrete: students taught, tickets resolved, hours saved. See [quantifying achievements](/articles/how-to-quantify-achievements-on-resume/).

## Myth 10: "An AI-written resume will be detected and rejected"

**Reality:** AI detection is unreliable. The real problem is that generic AI text reads as empty. Specific facts read as genuine. See [using ChatGPT for your resume](/articles/use-chatgpt-to-write-resume/).
