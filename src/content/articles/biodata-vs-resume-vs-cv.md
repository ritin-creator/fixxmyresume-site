## The three side by side

| | Biodata | Resume | CV |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Used in India for | Some government jobs, marriage proposals | Private-sector jobs | Same as resume in India; academic CV for research |
| Length | 1–2 pages | 1–2 pages | 1–2 pages (academic: longer) |
| Focus | Personal details | Skills, experience, achievements | Experience and achievements (academic: full record) |
| Includes DOB, religion, family | Yes | No | No |
| Photo | Often | Usually no | Usually no |
| Declaration | Often | No | No |

## Why biodata fell out of corporate hiring

Recruiters want to know what you can do, not your family details. Personal details take space, slow down screening and can introduce bias. Applicant tracking systems also have no use for them.

## What to send when

- **Private company, startup, MNC:** resume. See [resume format for freshers](/articles/resume-format-for-freshers-india/) or [for experienced professionals](/articles/resume-format-for-experienced-india/).
- **Government jobs:** follow the official application form exactly; it usually collects personal details itself.
- **Jobs abroad:** a CV in that country's format. See [resume for jobs abroad](/articles/resume-for-jobs-abroad-from-india/).
- **Academic or research posts:** an academic CV with publications.

## What each one looks like

A typical **biodata** opens with personal details and a declaration:

```
BIODATA
Name: ...           Father's name: ...
Date of birth: ...  Religion: ...  Marital status: ...
Address: ...
Educational qualifications: ...
Declaration: I hereby declare that the above information is true...
```

A **resume** for a private-sector job opens with what you offer:

```
PRIYA SHARMA
Pune | +91 98XXX XXXXX | priya.sharma@email.com | linkedin.com/in/priyasharma
Accounts executive with 3 years in GST, TDS and month-end closing.
EXPERIENCE ...
EDUCATION ...
SKILLS ...
```

An **academic CV** keeps going: research interests, publications, conferences, teaching, grants and referees, often across several pages.

## Government job applications

For central and state government jobs, you usually don't send any of the three. You fill the official online form, which asks for personal details, category, education and experience in fixed fields. Some interview stages ask for a "biodata" or "resume" too; in that case, follow the exact format the notification gives, because it varies by department.

## Jobs abroad

Most countries expect a resume or CV without biodata details. The UK and India use "CV" for the normal 1–2 page document; the US says "resume". The Gulf often expects nationality, visa status and sometimes a photo. See [resume for Gulf jobs](/articles/gulf-job-resume-uae-saudi/) and the [country-by-country photo guide](/articles/photo-on-resume-by-country/).

## Converting an old biodata into a resume

1. Delete father's name, religion, caste, marital status, full address and the declaration.
2. Keep name, phone, email and city at the top.
3. Add a two-line summary naming the role you want.
4. Turn job details into bullets that start with a verb and end with a result.
5. Keep education and skills; move them below experience if you have more than two years of work.
6. Save as a text-based PDF and check it with a free resume check.

## "My employer asked for a biodata"

Some smaller firms still use the word loosely. Send your resume and add only the personal details they specifically asked for, such as date of birth or languages known.
