## The three side by side

| | Biodata | Resume | CV |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Used in India for | Some government jobs, marriage proposals | Private-sector jobs | Same as resume in India; academic CV for research |
| Length | 1–2 pages | 1–2 pages | 1–2 pages (academic: longer) |
| Focus | Personal details | Skills, experience, achievements | Experience and achievements (academic: full record) |
| Includes DOB, religion, family | Yes | No | No |
| Photo | Often | Usually no | Usually no |
| Declaration | Often | No | No |

## Why biodata fell out of corporate hiring

Recruiters want to know what you can do, not your family details. Personal details take space, slow down screening and can introduce bias. Applicant tracking systems also have no use for them.

## What to send when

- **Private company, startup, MNC:** resume. See [resume format for freshers](/articles/resume-format-for-freshers-india/) or [for experienced professionals](/articles/resume-format-for-experienced-india/).
- **Government jobs:** follow the official application form exactly; it usually collects personal details itself.
- **Jobs abroad:** a CV in that country's format. See [resume for jobs abroad](/articles/resume-for-jobs-abroad-from-india/).
- **Academic or research posts:** an academic CV with publications.

## "My employer asked for a biodata"

Some smaller firms still use the word loosely. Send your resume and add only the personal details they specifically asked for, such as date of birth or languages known.
