A cover letter answers the question a resume can't: why you, for this job, now.

## The four-part structure

**1. Opening (1–2 sentences).** The role, where you saw it, and a reason you're interested that shows you know the company.

**2. Proof (2–3 sentences).** Your two strongest, most relevant achievements, with numbers.

**3. Fit (2 sentences).** Connect those achievements to what the job description says they need.

**4. Close (1–2 sentences).** Ask for a conversation. Thank them.

## Template

> Dear [Name or Hiring Manager],
>
> I'm applying for the [Job title] role at [Company]. [One specific reason: a product, a market move, a value you share.]
>
> In my current role as [title] at [company], I [achievement with number]. Before that, I [second achievement with number].
>
> Your advert mentions [requirement 1] and [requirement 2]. [How your experience covers them, briefly.]
>
> I'd welcome the chance to talk about how I could help [team or goal]. Thank you for your time.
>
> [Name] | [Phone] | [Email]

## Rules

- **Short.** Under 250 words.
- **Specific.** One concrete detail about the company beats three adjectives.
- **New information.** Don't retell your resume; pick the two most relevant points and add context.
- **Named recipient** if the advert or LinkedIn shows one.
- **No clichés.** "I am writing to express my interest…" wastes your first line.
