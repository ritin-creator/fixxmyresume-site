A cover letter answers the question a resume can't: why you, for this job, now.

## The four-part structure

**1. Opening (1–2 sentences).** The role, where you saw it, and a reason you're interested that shows you know the company.

**2. Proof (2–3 sentences).** Your two strongest, most relevant achievements, with numbers.

**3. Fit (2 sentences).** Connect those achievements to what the job description says they need.

**4. Close (1–2 sentences).** Ask for a conversation. Thank them.

## Template

> Dear [Name or Hiring Manager],
>
> I'm applying for the [Job title] role at [Company]. [One specific reason: a product, a market move, a value you share.]
>
> In my current role as [title] at [company], I [achievement with number]. Before that, I [second achievement with number].
>
> Your advert mentions [requirement 1] and [requirement 2]. [How your experience covers them, briefly.]
>
> I'd welcome the chance to talk about how I could help [team or goal]. Thank you for your time.
>
> [Name] | [Phone] | [Email]

## A worked example

Here is the template filled in for a real-looking job: an accounts executive applying to a consumer goods company. The comments in brackets explain each choice.

> Dear Ms Mehta,
>
> I'm applying for the Accounts Executive role at Sunrise Foods that I saw on your careers page. Your expansion into three new states this year means more GST registrations and vendors to manage, which is exactly the work I enjoy. *(Names the role, shows they read about the company, links it to the job.)*
>
> At Patel Industries I handle GST returns for two state registrations and about 120 vendors. I cut our monthly bank reconciliation from four days to two by building an Excel tracker, and our last statutory audit closed with no GST observations. *(Two achievements with numbers.)*
>
> Your advert asks for GST compliance across states and experience with Tally Prime. I've filed GSTR-1 and GSTR-3B for both our registrations for three years, all in Tally Prime. *(Matches their two requirements directly.)*
>
> I'd welcome the chance to talk about supporting your expansion. Thank you for your time.
>
> Rohan Verma | +91 98XXX XXXXX | rohan.verma@email.com

That's about 160 words. Nothing in it is a generic line that could go to any company.

## Tailor it in ten minutes

1. **Read the job ad twice** and underline the two requirements that matter most.
2. **Find one real detail** about the company: a recent launch, expansion, customer, value or news item.
3. **Pick your two best matching achievements** from your resume, with numbers.
4. **Write the four parts** using the template, then cut anything that doesn't serve the job.
5. **Read it aloud.** If a sentence sounds like a form letter, rewrite it in plain words.

## Email or attachment?

If you apply by email, the email body is your cover letter. Keep it shorter (100–150 words), put the role in the subject line (`Application: Accounts Executive – Rohan Verma`) and attach your resume as a PDF. If a portal has a cover letter box, paste the text in; if it asks for a file, upload a PDF.

## Special situations

- **Career change:** use the fit paragraph to connect skills from your old field to the new one, with one example.
- **Employment gap:** don't explain it in detail; one sentence is enough if it's recent, such as "After a year caring for a family member, I'm returning to full-time work."
- **No named contact:** "Dear Hiring Manager" is fine. Avoid "To whom it may concern".
- **Referral:** mention the person in the first line: "Anjali Rao in your finance team suggested I apply."

## Rules

- **Short.** Under 250 words.
- **Specific.** One concrete detail about the company beats three adjectives.
- **New information.** Don't retell your resume; pick the two most relevant points and add context.
- **Named recipient** if the advert or LinkedIn shows one.
- **No clichés.** "I am writing to express my interest…" wastes your first line.
